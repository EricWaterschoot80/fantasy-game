Why point-and-click games suit children

A point-and-click adventure is a story you move forward yourself. You look around a drawn scene, tap whatever seems interesting, talk to the characters and work out which item helps where. Nothing happens on a timer, so a child can think as long as they need.

That slow pace is the point. Instead of quick reactions, these games reward looking carefully, reading what characters say and trying an idea to see if it works. A puzzle usually has a small, logical answer: if a dog won't let go of a key, what might it want instead, and where could that be?

Each game on this site is short, around 15 to 20 minutes, which fits in one sitting and gives a clear ending instead of an endless loop.

Playing together

  • Read along

    For children who are still learning to read, read the characters' lines aloud and let them choose where to tap.

  • Think out loud

    Ask what they think an item is for before they try it. Guessing and checking is half the fun.

  • Try the other language

    Every game has English and Dutch. Playing a game they already know in the other language is an easy way to practise.

  • Stuck? Look again

    Tap every place in the scene once more and try each item on things. The answer is almost always something already on screen.

Questions parents ask

What age are point-and-click games suitable for?

It depends more on reading than on age. As a guide, Maanhoef and The Amulet of Emberfall suit children from about 7, and Whispers of Ravenholt from about 9. Younger children can play with a parent reading along, and the games are made for adults as much as for kids.

Are there ads or in-app purchases?

No. The games on RetroAdventureWorld are free, with no adverts, no in-app purchases and no chat with other players.

Do the games work on a tablet or phone?

Yes. They run in the browser on tablets, phones and computers. On a phone, turn it sideways for the best view.

Can my child play in Dutch or English?

Yes. Every game has English and Dutch, so children can play in their own language or use the other one to practise.
